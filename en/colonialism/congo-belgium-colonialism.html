🩸 Congo Under Belgian Colonialism

1885-1908 – The Largest Genocide in Colonial History

1885 – 1908 Congo Basin – Central Africa Reading time: 13 minutes 10 Million Dead

📑 Table of Contents

In the heart of Africa, in the rainforests of the Congo, one of the largest mass killings in human history took place. Not by Hitler. Not by Stalin. But by the King of Belgium: Leopold II. This man never visited the Congo. But he owned it. Literally. 2.3 million square kilometers (80 times the size of Belgium) became his private property. He called it the "Congo Free State." Free? No. It was a vast prison. The goal: rubber. In the late 19th century, the rubber tire was invented. The world needed rubber. And Congo was filled with rubber trees. Leopold turned the Congolese into slaves. Those who failed to meet their rubber quotas... had their hands cut off. 10 million human beings died between 1885 and 1908. A forgotten genocide. This is its story.

10,000,000

That's more than the entire population of Belgium today.

Half the Congolese population was exterminated in just 23 years.

Summary: Between 1885 and 1908, the "Congo Free State" was the private property of Belgian King Leopold II. The local population was enslaved to collect rubber. Punishments: severed hands, flogging to death, burning villages. Between 5-10 million people died (half the population). In 1908, international pressure forced Belgium to annex the Congo as an official colony. Congo (Zaire) became independent in 1960.

👑 Leopold II: The Greedy King Who Bought a Country

Leopold II (1835-1909) was King of Belgium. But Belgium did not want colonies. So Leopold decided to buy a colony for himself. Using shell companies and humanitarian fronts, he sent explorer Henry Morton Stanley to the Congo Basin. Stanley signed "treaties" with tribal chiefs (who could not read and did not understand what they were signing). At the Berlin Conference of 1885, Europe recognized the "Congo Free State" as Leopold's private property. Leopold promised to protect the natives and end the slave trade. Instead... he began the largest system of forced labor in human history. He never set foot in Africa.

🩸 Rubber and Blood: The System of Terror

The invention of the rubber tire (1888) by John Dunlop made rubber the new "black gold." And Congo was full of wild rubber vines. Leopold established the "Force Publique" – a brutal militia of white mercenaries and African conscripts. Their orders: every village must produce a quota of rubber. Those who failed... were punished. The punishment: severed hands. Soldiers cut off the hands of Congolese men, women, and children. To prove they hadn't wasted bullets on hunting, they returned from the jungle with baskets full of severed hands. They smoked them to preserve them as proof. Entire villages were burned to the ground. Women were raped. Children were taken as hostages until villages met their rubber quotas. Men who couldn't work were shot or had their hands cut off.

"Every piece of rubber in the Congo is stained with blood."

— Edmund Dene Morel, British journalist and activist, 1904

📸 The Whistleblowers: Exposing the Crime to the World

But the crime did not go unnoticed. The world eventually found out. British and American missionaries began reporting the atrocities. Roger Casement (a British diplomat) published a devastating report in 1904 detailing the crimes. Edmund Dene Morel founded the "Congo Reform Association" – the first mass human rights campaign of the 20th century. Mark Twain wrote a satirical pamphlet: "King Leopold's Soliloquy" – imagining the king defending his crimes. Photographs spread across newspapers worldwide: children without hands, men without feet, fathers holding their daughters' severed limbs. World public opinion exploded with anger. In 1908, the Belgian parliament forced Leopold to "sell" the Congo to Belgium for 50 million francs. Leopold died in 1909, unrepentant. He said before his death: "I will go down in history knowing I did what was best for the Congo."

The Richest Man in the World: At the height of his reign, Leopold II was arguably the richest man on Earth. His personal fortune (adjusted for inflation) would be worth approximately $1.2 billion today. All built on the bones of 10 million Congolese.

🏛️ Belgium's Official Colonialism (1908-1960)

After Leopold was forced to hand over the Congo in 1908, Belgium continued to rule the colony until 1960. The brutal forced labor system was replaced with slightly less brutal exploitation. Belgians controlled the mines (copper, cobalt, uranium), plantations, and administration. Congolese were still denied education beyond basic levels. At independence in 1960, there were only 16 Congolese university graduates in the entire country of 15 million people. Belgium left behind a population traumatized, uneducated, and unprepared for self-governance. The chaos that followed – the assassination of Patrice Lumumba, the rise of Mobutu Sese Seko, and decades of dictatorship – can all be traced directly back to the Belgian colonial system. The wounds have never fully healed.

🗿 The Silence of History: Why This Genocide is Forgotten

Why is this genocide not taught in most schools? Why do history books largely ignore it? Several reasons: 1) Belgium actively destroyed evidence. In 1908, when they took over the Congo, they burned the archives of the Congo Free State for 8 days. 2) The victims were African. In the racist worldview of early 20th century Europe, African lives did not count the same as European ones. 3) European powers had their own colonial crimes to hide. If Belgium was condemned too harshly, what about British India? French Algeria? German South-West Africa? The silence was convenient for everyone. 4) The Congo's post-independence troubles under Mobutu made it easy for the West to dismiss the country as "naturally chaotic" – ignoring the colonial roots of that chaos. Today, a statue of Leopold II still stands in Brussels. It has been vandalized repeatedly. But it still stands.
