🐍 Cleopatra VII

The Last Pharaoh - Egypt's Most Famous Queen

69 - 30 BC Alexandria - Egypt Reading time: 12 minutes 22 years on the throne

Cleopatra VII (69-30 BC) is the most famous woman in ancient history. The last ruler of Ptolemaic Egypt (the Greek dynasty that ruled Egypt after Alexander the Great). But she was not Egyptian! She was Greek Macedonian (from the Ptolemaic dynasty, one of Alexander's generals). However, she was the first Ptolemaic ruler to learn the Egyptian language. She ruled for 22 years (51-30 BC). She had love and political affairs with the two most powerful men in Rome: Julius Caesar (with whom she had a son) and Mark Antony (with whom she had 3 children). She dreamed of a great Eastern empire. But she lost everything at the naval Battle of Actium (31 BC). She committed suicide in 30 BC at age 39. With her death, Pharaonic Egypt (3,000 years of history) ended and became a Roman province.

Summary: Cleopatra VII (69-30 BC). Greek (Ptolemaic), but identified as Egyptian. Spoke 9 languages. Ruled Egypt 22 years. Affair with Julius Caesar (47-44 BC): bore his son Caesarion. Affair with Mark Antony (41-30 BC): bore 3 children. Dreamed of an Eastern-Roman empire. Defeated at the Battle of Actium (31 BC) against Octavian (later Caesar Augustus). Committed suicide August 12, 30 BC (age 39). With her died Pharaonic Egypt.

🧠 A Political Genius, Not Just a Beauty

The stereotype of Cleopatra: a beautiful seductress who used her body to ensnare men. This is Roman propaganda. The truth: she was not exceptionally beautiful. Coins bearing her image show a woman with sharp features, a prominent nose. But she was brilliant: she spoke 9 languages (Greek, Egyptian, Hebrew, Aramaic, Syriac, Median, Parthian, Latin, Arabic). She wrote books on medicine, mathematics, and cosmetics. She was a shrewd politician and a captivating orator. Plutarch (the Greek historian) wrote about her: "Her beauty was not stunning. But her charm, her intelligence, and her voice were captivating." She captivated Caesar with her mind, not her body.

"I will never be a piece in Rome's chess game. I will be the player."

— Words attributed to Cleopatra

💔 Mark Antony: The Love That Destroyed Her

After Caesar's assassination (44 BC), Cleopatra needed a new protector. Mark Antony — one of Rome's three rulers — was in the East. In 41 BC, he summoned her to Tarsus (Turkey). She arrived on a golden ship, with purple sails, silver oars, music, perfumes, and servants dressed as gods. It was not just a political visit. It was a dazzling display of wealth and power. Antony fell in love with her. He moved to Alexandria. He gave her Roman territories. He recognized her children as his own. In 34 BC, he declared the division of the East: he gave her and Caesarion the title "King and Queen of Kings." This infuriated Rome. Octavian (Caesar's adopted son, Antony's rival) used this against him: "Antony has betrayed Rome for an Eastern whore." War became inevitable.

The Suicide: August 12, 30 BC

"After the defeat, Octavian besieged Alexandria. Antony — believing Cleopatra was dead — stabbed himself with his sword. He was carried to her. He died in her arms. Octavian wanted to parade her through the streets of Rome (chained in his triumph). She refused this humiliation. On August 12, 30 BC, she took her final bath, put on her most beautiful royal robes, and opened a basket of figs... containing a poisonous cobra (or used poison hidden in a hairpin). She died with her two handmaidens. Octavian was furious (he lost his prize). But he admired her courage. She was buried beside Antony. Her son Caesarion (age 17) was executed. Her children by Antony (twins, age 10) were raised in the house of Octavia (Antony's former Roman wife!). A tragic end to the greatest woman of the ancient world."
