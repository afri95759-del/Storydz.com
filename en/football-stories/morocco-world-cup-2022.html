🇲🇦 Morocco at the 2022 World Cup

The Atlas Lions — The Pride of Africa and the Arab World

November 20 – December 18, 2022 Qatar Reading time: 14 minutes First African and Arab team to reach the World Cup semifinals

In the winter of 2022, the Atlas Lions of Morocco achieved something that had never been done before in the history of the World Cup: they became the first African team — and the first Arab team — to reach the semifinals of football's greatest tournament. Their journey was not a fluke or a lucky run. It was a masterclass in tactical discipline, defensive steel, and the sheer unifying power of sport. Morocco didn't just win; they beat Belgium (the world's number two-ranked team), eliminated Spain on penalties, and knocked out Cristiano Ronaldo's Portugal — all without conceding a single goal from open play until their semifinal defeat to France. Their goalkeeper, Yassine Bounou, became a folk hero. Their right-back, Achraf Hakimi, scored the Panenka penalty that sent Spain home. Their coach, Walid Regragui — a Moroccan-born Frenchman who had only been in charge for three months before the tournament — became an icon. The Moroccan team's success electrified the Arab world, the African continent, and diaspora communities across Europe. When Hakimi ran to the stands to kiss his mother after every victory, when Sofiane Boufal danced with his mother on the pitch, when the red-and-green flag of Morocco was waved from Ramallah to the Champs-Élysées — the 2022 World Cup became something greater than football. It was a declaration: the global South, the former colonies, the Arab world, and the African continent belonged on the biggest stage. This is the story of the month that changed Moroccan football forever.

Summary: Morocco's performance at the 2022 FIFA World Cup in Qatar was historic. Coached by Walid Regragui, Morocco topped Group F ahead of Croatia, Belgium, and Canada. They defeated Spain 3-0 on penalties in the round of 16. In the quarterfinals, they beat Portugal 1-0. In the semifinals, they fell 2-0 to France. They lost the third-place match 2-1 to Croatia. They became the first African and first Arab nation to reach the World Cup semifinals. Key players included goalkeeper Yassine Bounou, right-back Achraf Hakimi (PSG), midfielder Sofyan Amrabat, and winger Hakim Ziyech.

👔 Regragui: The Man Who Unified a Team

Walid Regragui was a former Moroccan international defender, born in France, with a modest coaching career. He was appointed just three months before the World Cup after the firing of Vahid Halilhodzic, who had clashed with star players Ziyech and Noussair Mazraoui. Regragui immediately recalled the exiled stars, mended the fractured dressing room, and instilled a simple philosophy: "We will defend like lions." His 4-1-4-1 system transformed Morocco into a defensive wall. They conceded only once from open play in the entire tournament. Regragui was charismatic, media-savvy, and deeply loved by his players.

The Penalty Shootout — Spain, December 6, 2022

"120 minutes. 0-0. Bounou vs. Unai Simon. The first Spanish penalty — saved. The second — hit the post. The third — saved. Morocco had two chances. Hakimi stepped up. The entire Arab world held its breath. He chipped the ball — a Panenka — into the center of the goal. Spain was eliminated. Morocco erupted."

🦁 The Matches

The campaign began with a goalless draw against 2018 runners-up Croatia. Then came the stunning 2-0 victory over Belgium — the world's number-two-ranked team. Belgium's "golden generation" was humbled; the Atlas Lions roared. A 2-1 win over Canada sealed top spot in Group F. In the last 16, Morocco faced Spain. For 120 minutes, the Spanish passed and probed, but they could not penetrate the red-and-green wall. Bounou was a giant in goal. The penalty shootout was a cauldron of tension. Morocco won 3-0 on penalties. In the quarterfinals, a towering header from Youssef En-Nesyri stunned Portugal. Cristiano Ronaldo — the tournament's biggest star — left the field in tears. Morocco had reached the semifinals.

💔 The Semifinal and the Third-Place Match

France — the defending champions — proved a bridge too far. Theo Hernández scored early. Morocco fought magnificently but lost 2-0. The dream of the final was over, but the team had already made history. In the third-place match against Croatia, Morocco lost 2-1 but won the hearts of the world. When they returned to Rabat, they were greeted by a sea of red and green — hundreds of thousands of Moroccans lining the streets in an open-top bus parade.

📖 The Legacy: A Continent Believed Again

Morocco's 2022 run was more than a football story. It was a Pan-African and Pan-Arab moment. The Moroccan team represented not just a nation but a diaspora — players born in France, the Netherlands, Belgium, Spain, and Italy, returning to the land of their parents and carrying a continent's hopes on their shoulders. The images of Hakimi kissing his mother, of Boufal dancing with his mother on the pitch, of Palestinians waving Moroccan flags — these became the enduring images of Qatar 2022. Walid Regragui said it best: "Before the tournament, we were told you can't win with African mentality. We proved that you can go far with African players, an African coach, and African spirit."
