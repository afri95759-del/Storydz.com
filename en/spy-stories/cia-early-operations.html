🕵️ CIA Early Operations 1947-1961

Coups, Mind Control, and the Dawn of the Cold War

1947 - 1961 Iran, Guatemala, Cuba, Europe Reading time: 14 minutes The Allen Dulles Era

On September 18, 1947, President Harry Truman signed the National Security Act that created the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA). Its official mission: gather and analyze intelligence. But what happened next was something completely different. Within just 15 years of its creation, the CIA transformed from a desk-bound analytical agency into a global covert action machine: it overthrew elected governments, carried out assassinations, funded proxy wars, and conducted secret mind-control experiments on American citizens. This was the "golden age" of the CIA — the era of Allen Dulles (agency director 1953-1961). During this period, the CIA became a state within a state: secret budgets (no Congressional oversight), unlimited powers, and near-complete immunity. This is the story of the first operations that shaped the most powerful intelligence agency in history.

Summary: CIA founded 1947. Most famous director: Allen Dulles (1953-1961). Most famous early operations: Iran coup 1953 (overthrow of Mohammad Mossadegh, reinstalling the Shah), Guatemala coup 1954 (overthrow of Jacobo Árbenz, 36-year bloody civil war), Bay of Pigs invasion of Cuba 1961 (disastrous failure), MKUltra program (mind control and drug experiments on humans), espionage operations in Eastern Europe. Criticisms: controlled American foreign policy, interfered in sovereign nations, supported brutal dictatorships in the name of "fighting communism."

🇮🇷 Iran Coup 1953: Operation Ajax

This was the CIA's first major coup operation. In 1951, Iranian Prime Minister Mohammad Mossadegh nationalized the Anglo-Iranian Oil Company (which Britain controlled and which was plundering Iran's oil wealth). Britain was furious and asked for American help. The CIA (led by Kermit Roosevelt — grandson of President Theodore Roosevelt) planned Operation Ajax. Budget: $1 million. The plan: bribe journalists, politicians, and clerics, organize violent demonstrations, spread rumors that Mossadegh was "crazy," then incite the army to revolt. In August 1953, the coup succeeded. Mossadegh was arrested (3 years in prison, then house arrest until his death). Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi returned to the throne (a pro-Western dictator). The result: 25 years of brutal repression (SAVAK — the secret police). Then the 1979 Islamic Revolution that brought Ayatollah Khomeini to power. The CIA later regretted this coup: "We created a bigger enemy." But the lesson was not learned.

🇬🇹 Guatemala Coup 1954: Bananas vs. Communism

In 1953, Jacobo Árbenz was elected president of Guatemala. He was a reformer (not a communist). He distributed uncultivated land to poor farmers. The problem: part of this land belonged to the giant American United Fruit Company (which controlled 42% of Guatemala's land, paid negligible taxes, and controlled the railways and ports). The company went to Washington. Secretary of State John Foster Dulles (brother of CIA Director Allen Dulles) was a former lawyer for United Fruit. Allen Dulles himself was a board member of the company! Blatant conflict of interest. Operation PBSUCCESS overthrew Árbenz: media propaganda, CIA planes bombed the capital, a small mercenary army invaded from Honduras. Árbenz resigned and fled. The result: brutal military rule, then a 36-year civil war that killed 200,000 people (mostly indigenous Maya). In Latin America, the word "banana" became synonymous with a US-backed dictatorship.

"We acted as if God had granted us the right to decide the fate of other nations. We believed we could change the world with secret money and coups. But every coup planted the seeds of a future disaster."

— Former CIA official, 1990

🧠 MKUltra: Mind Control and Human Experiments

In 1953, the CIA began a shocking secret program: MKUltra. The goal: develop mind control techniques (brainwashing, interrogation, drugging). The agency tested hallucinogenic drugs on hundreds of people without their knowledge or consent: prisoners, mental patients, drug addicts, and even brothel patrons (the CIA ran brothels in San Francisco and New York to lure unsuspecting individuals and test drugs on them). Most famous case: Frank Olson — a CIA scientist — jumped from a hotel window in 1953 after being secretly dosed with LSD (he became psychotic). In 1973, CIA Director Richard Helms destroyed all MKUltra documents. Only fragments remain. The program was exposed in the 1970s (Congressional investigation). But the full truth remains buried. Thousands of victims never knew they were guinea pigs in one of the most horrifying intelligence operations in American history.

Bay of Pigs: Anatomy of Failure

"The CIA's basic assumption was that the Cuban people would rise up against Castro as soon as the invaders landed. This was hope, not intelligence analysis. Castro was widely popular (especially among the poor who benefited from his reforms). The invasion failed militarily and politically. But the bigger lesson: the CIA had become too confident in itself. Its success in Iran and Guatemala convinced it that it could overthrow any government. The Bay of Pigs was the slap that awakened it from its arrogance. But it soon returned to its old patterns: Vietnam, Chile, Nicaragua... the story repeats."
